What to Ask Before Hiring a House Cleaner in Berks County

October 19, 2026 5 min read

You're handing someone a key to your house, or at least an unsupervised hour inside it. That's worth a few direct questions before you book, not just a price comparison. Here's what actually matters, and why, from someone who does this work in Berks County every day.

Who is actually coming to my house?

Ask whether you'll get the same person or team every visit, or a rotating crew assigned by whoever's available that day. Neither answer is automatically wrong, but it changes what you should expect. A consistent cleaner learns which cabinet the good pan lives in, which houseplant not to move, and which corner of the kitchen always needs extra attention. A rotating crew resets that knowledge every visit.

What's included, and what costs extra?

This is where price comparisons between companies get misleading. A regular cleaning typically covers kitchen counters, sinks, stovetops, bathroom fixtures, mirrors, floors, dusting, and general tidying. Baseboards, window tracks, inside the oven, and inside the fridge are usually add-ons or part of a deep clean, not standard. Get the exact list before you compare a $150 quote to a $110 one, since the cheaper one may simply be covering less ground.

Ask for it in writing

A real quote reflects your home's actual size and condition. If a company gives you a flat number before seeing or hearing details about your home, ask how they arrived at it. A walkthrough, photos, or at minimum a real conversation about square footage and room count should shape the price.

Who supplies the equipment and products?

Some services bring their own vacuum, mop, and cleaning products. Others expect you to provide them, or charge extra if they have to bring their own. Ask directly, since this affects both the price and the quality of the clean, a commercial-grade vacuum and floor machine pick up more than a standard household model.

What's the policy if something gets broken or a visit needs to move?

Accidents happen even with careful cleaners. Ask what happens if something is damaged, and ask about the cancellation or rescheduling policy before you need it, not after. A company that's upfront about both is usually the same kind of company that's upfront about pricing.

Are they insured?

This is worth asking every company you consider, including any you're currently working with. Ask to see proof rather than taking a verbal yes, and factor the answer into your decision alongside price and availability.

Putting it together

None of these questions are complicated, but asking all of them before you book tells you more than a star rating or a price alone. A company that answers directly and specifically is usually the one that'll show up and do consistent work. One that gets vague or defensive about any of these is worth a second look before you commit.

Frequently asked questions

Should I get a written quote before booking?

Yes. A real quote should reflect your home's actual size and condition, not a flat rate pulled from a website. If a company won't give you a number until after you've booked, that's worth asking about directly.

Is it normal for the same cleaner to come every time?

It varies by company. Some send a rotating crew, others send the same person or team every visit. Neither is automatically wrong, but you should know which one you're getting, since a consistent cleaner learns your home's specific needs over time.

What's usually not included in a standard cleaning?

Detail work like baseboards, window tracks, inside the oven, and inside the fridge are typically add-ons or part of a deep clean rather than a regular visit. Ask what's included before you compare prices between companies, since a lower price sometimes just means less is covered.
